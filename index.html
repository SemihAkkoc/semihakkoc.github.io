I’m a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Maryland, College Park, advised by Prof. Sennur Ulukus.

My research has two sides. On the compression side, I derive rate–distortion characterizations for task-aware compression: how many bits you need when the reconstruction is consumed by a downstream model rather than a person, and how far a lossy codec can steer the statistics of what it outputs. On the interpretability side, I analyze transformers as tractable dynamical systems, treating tokens as interacting particles to find where the representational work inside a network actually happens.

Before Maryland, I received my B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronics Engineering from Bilkent University, with an exchange semester at the National University of Singapore. At Bilkent I worked on semantic signal processing with Prof. Orhan Arıkan and on computational electromagnetics with Prof. Vakur B. Ertürk. I’ve also interned at Huawei (semantic communications), ASELSAN (RF & microwave systems), and BİTES (software engineering).

02 · Research

Current Focus

My research establishes the fundamental limits of what can be discarded: deriving rate–distortion characterizations that govern task-aware compression, and analyzing transformers as tractable dynamical systems to locate where representational work actually happens.

Task-aware compression

Rate–distortion limits for compression whose output is consumed by a downstream model, including classifiers trained on a different distribution than the source.

Transformers as dynamical systems

Treating tokens as interacting particles to analyze attention and feed-forward blocks, and to find where the compressive work inside a network happens.

Steering output statistics

How far a lossy codec can steer the statistics of what it outputs while staying faithful to the source, with uses in watermarking, output auditing, and privacy.

03 · Publications

Papers & Preprints

  1. Accepted
    The Rate–Distortion–Deception Tradeoff
    S. Akkoc, S. Liyanaarachchi, S. Ulukus, and A. Yener
    62nd Allerton Conference on Communication, Control, and Computing · Sep 2026

    Introduces deception as a third axis of the rate–distortion–perception framework: a compressed reconstruction stays within a target fidelity of the source while provably resembling a sample from a different distribution. Relevant to generative-model watermarking, output auditing, and privacy-preserving compression.

    arXiv Bib

    S. Akkoc†, A. Çınar†, B. Ercan†, M. Kalfa, and O. Arıkan
    Journal of the Franklin Institute, vol. 362, no. 1 · 2024

    † Equal contribution

    A hardware realization of a goal-oriented semantic communication network, showing that low-power commodity edge devices can sustain real-time semantic extraction and multi-sensor fusion.

  3. Under Review
    On the Diverse Dynamical Behaviors Arising in Deep Linear Transformers
    S. Li, T. J. Maranzatto, J. Peszek, T. Teolis, S. Akkoc, K. Riedl, S. Ulukus, and N. García Trillos
    Foundations of Computational Mathematics (FoCM) · Jul 2026

    Recasts transformer inference as an interacting-particle system and proves that, in embedding dimension two, the depth-wise dynamics reduce exactly to a generalized Kuramoto model, giving a closed-form surrogate for stacked linear self-attention and explaining token clustering, oscillation, and bifurcation.

    arXiv Bib
  4. Submitted
    Task-aware Compression with Distribution Matching
    S. Liyanaarachchi, S. Akkoc, S. Ulukus, and A. Yener
    ICLR 2027 · Sep 2026

    Derives the optimal compression rate when the reconstruction is consumed by a mismatched downstream classifier, and recasts the design as a rate–distortion–perception problem with a KL constraint matching the classifier’s training distribution.

    arXiv Bib
  5. Submitted
    The Role of Feed-Forward Layers in Transformer Dynamics
    T. J. Maranzatto, S. Akkoc, and S. Ulukus
    AISTATS 2027 · Sep 2026

    Extends the interacting-particle analysis of transformers beyond attention to the feed-forward block, isolating how pointwise nonlinearities reshape token trajectories and which layers carry the compressive work of the network.
