Understanding catastrophic forgetting during sequential fine-tuning

Why a network that learns a new task loses an old one, what decides how much it loses, and how to prevent it, read through the gradients that training already computes.

Semih Akkoç · ECE Ph.D., University of Maryland · Instructor: Prof. Ming C. Lin

Running example

Two tasks, two parameters

Each task is one linear constraint, so its solutions form a line in the plane. The model is small enough to draw, and it already shows every effect in this lecture: forgetting, the role of similarity, and what each fix does.

{% include section_header.liquid n=1 label='The phenomenon' title='1989: a network learns the twos and forgets the ones' %}
  • A backprop network learned single-digit addition facts with a 1 (1+1, 2+1, …), then was trained only on facts with a 2.
  • It learned the twos easily, but after a few training passes on them it got most of the ones wrong.
  • People in similar list-learning experiments forget gradually. The network forgot far more, far faster: catastrophic interference.
  • The underlying tension is the stability–plasticity dilemma: weights that are free to learn are also free to forget.
McCloskey & Cohen 1989 · Ratcliff 1990 · French 1999
{% include section_header.liquid n=1 label='The phenomenon' title='The setup, and how forgetting is measured' %}
$$\theta_0 \xrightarrow{\ \text{task 1}\ } \theta^\star \xrightarrow{\ \text{task 2},\ T\text{ steps}\ } \theta_T$$
$$F(T) = \mathcal{L}_1(\theta_T) - \mathcal{L}_1(\theta^\star)$$

Forgetting is the rise in the old task's loss (or the drop in its accuracy) caused by the new training.

Keep in mind

  • Three settings: task-, domain- and class-incremental; the last is hardest.
  • In LLM fine-tuning task 1 is everything the model already knew, measured on held-out benchmarks.
  • Report learning with forgetting. A model that never moves never forgets, so compare on a learning–forgetting frontier.
  • Upper bound: joint training on all tasks at once.
van de Ven & Tolias 2019
{% include section_header.liquid n=2 label='Why it happens' title='Not a capacity problem: the toy model' %}

Task \(k\): \(\mathcal{L}_k(\theta) = \tfrac12(u_k^\top\theta - 1)^2\), so its solutions form a line. Joint training would find the crossing, which solves both tasks. Fine-tuning stops elsewhere.

{% include section_header.liquid n=2 label='Why it happens' title='Forgetting is accumulated gradient conflict' %}

Fine-tune by gradient flow, \(\dot\theta = -\nabla\mathcal{L}_2(\theta)\), and apply the chain rule to task 1's loss:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{L}_1(\theta_t) = \nabla\mathcal{L}_1^{\top}\dot\theta_t = -\big\langle \nabla\mathcal{L}_1,\ \nabla\mathcal{L}_2 \big\rangle$$
\(\Longrightarrow\)
$$F(T) = -\int_0^T \big\langle \nabla\mathcal{L}_1(\theta_t),\ \nabla\mathcal{L}_2(\theta_t) \big\rangle\, dt$$

Forgetting is the negative alignment of the two tasks' gradients, accumulated along the fine-tuning path.

Aligned · \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle > 0\)

Task 1 improves too: positive transfer.

Orthogonal · \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle = 0\)

No first-order effect on task 1.

Conflicting · \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle < 0\)

Task 1's loss rises: forgetting.

Cost: two backward passes and a dot product. Split per weight, the terms \(-\partial_i\mathcal{L}_1\,\partial_i\mathcal{L}_2\) say where forgetting happens; Synaptic Intelligence (Zenke et al. 2017) accumulates the same path integral per weight.

In more detail

Discrete steps and other optimizers

With SGD at step size \(\eta\), a Taylor expansion of \(\mathcal{L}_1\) around the current point gives

$$\Delta\mathcal{L}_1 = -\eta\,\langle \nabla\mathcal{L}_1, \nabla\mathcal{L}_2 \rangle + \tfrac{\eta^2}{2}\,\nabla\mathcal{L}_2^{\top} H_1 \nabla\mathcal{L}_2 + O(\eta^3),$$

so the identity holds up to a curvature correction that grows with the step size. With a preconditioned optimizer whose update is \(-\eta P\nabla\mathcal{L}_2\) (Adam is approximately diagonal \(P\)), the first-order term becomes \(-\eta\,\nabla\mathcal{L}_1^{\top} P\, \nabla\mathcal{L}_2\): the alignment is measured in the optimizer's metric.

The sign of the integrand is exactly what gradient-projection methods control. GEM keeps it non-negative for every stored task, and OGD makes it zero to first order (part 4).

{% include section_header.liquid n=2 label='Why it happens' title='Near the old optimum, curvature decides' %}

Right after task 1, \(\nabla\mathcal{L}_1(\theta^\star) \approx 0\), so the integrand starts at zero. Expand to second order in \(\Delta\theta = \theta_T - \theta^\star\):

$$F \approx \tfrac12\,\Delta\theta^{\top} H_1\, \Delta\theta = \tfrac12 \sum_k \lambda_k\,\big(v_k^{\top}\Delta\theta\big)^2$$
  • Direction matters more than distance. Sharp directions (large \(\lambda_k\)) are expensive; flat ones are nearly free.
  • Network Hessians have a few large eigenvalues and a bulk near zero, so most directions are flat.
  • \(F \le \tfrac12\lambda_{\max}\|\Delta\theta\|^2\): flatter minima and smaller updates forget less.
  • Autodiff gives \(H_1 v\) for about the cost of two gradients. The Fisher information is a cheap stand-in for \(H_1\), which is where EWC comes from.
Sagun et al. 2017 · Mirzadeh et al. 2020 · Pearlmutter 1994
{% include section_header.liquid n=3 label='Task similarity' title='Forgetting peaks at intermediate similarity' %}

In the toy model, put the two tasks at angle \(\varphi\) and call \(c = \cos\varphi\) their similarity. Fine-tuning to convergence gives

$$F(c) = \tfrac12\,\underbrace{(1-c)^2}_{\text{movement}}\;\underbrace{c^2}_{\text{sensitivity}}$$
  • Identical tasks (\(c=1\)): nothing new to learn, so no movement.
  • Unrelated tasks (\(c=0\)): you move only where task 1 is blind.
  • In between, real movement along a direction task 1 depends on. The peak is at \(c=\tfrac12\).
  • The same shape appears in exact linear-regression theory, teacher–student networks and CIFAR classifiers.
Evron et al. 2022 · Lee et al. 2021 · Ramasesh et al. 2021

In more detail

Deriving the curve

Take \(u_1 = (1,0)\) and \(u_2 = (\cos\varphi, \sin\varphi)\). Gradient descent on task 1 from \(\theta_0 = 0\) moves only along \(u_1\) and stops at \(\theta^\star = u_1\). Gradient descent on task 2 from there moves only along \(u_2\) and stops at \(\theta_T = u_1 + (1-c)\,u_2\), the nearest point of the task-2 line. Then \(u_1^\top\theta_T - 1 = (1-c)\,c\), so \(F = \tfrac12 (1-c)^2 c^2\). Because the task loss is quadratic, the second-order formula \(\tfrac12\Delta\theta^\top H_1\Delta\theta\) with \(H_1 = u_1u_1^\top\) is exact here.

{% include section_header.liquid n=3 label='Task similarity' title='Teacher–student theory: similarity is not one number' %}
  • Setup. A two-layer student learns teacher A, then teacher B, by online SGD. In the high-dimensional limit its learning curves follow exact ODEs.
  • Features: forgetting is worst for intermediately similar teachers and small for aligned or orthogonal ones. Orthogonal teachers can live in separate hidden units; correlated ones are hard to separate.
  • Readouts act differently: high readout similarity makes forgetting start faster, and orthogonal features with aligned readouts give the smallest peak forgetting.
  • When you measure matters: initial, peak and long-run forgetting can rank the same task pairs differently.
  • Real networks agree: in image classifiers, forgetting peaks at intermediate similarity and sits mostly in the deeper layers.
Lee, Goldt & Saxe, ICML 2021 · Ramasesh et al., ICLR 2021
{% include section_header.liquid n=4 label='Preventing it' title='Four families of fixes, each constrains the update' %}
Protect · regularize

Penalize moving weights that mattered for old tasks (EWC, SI, MAS), or keep old outputs fixed by distillation (LwF).

Acts on the second-order term \(\tfrac12\Delta\theta^\top H_1\Delta\theta\)

Project · constrain the gradient

Remove the part of the update that conflicts with old tasks (GEM, A-GEM, OGD, GPM).

Acts on the sign of \(\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_1, \nabla\mathcal{L}_2\rangle\)

Replay · rehearse

Mix stored or generated old examples into training. For LLMs, mix pretraining data into fine-tuning.

Acts on the objective: adds \(\nabla\mathcal{L}_1\) back, approximating joint training

Isolate · architecture

Give new tasks their own parameters (progressive networks, PackNet, adapters, LoRA).

Acts on which directions can move at all

Every family buys remembering with some learning: the update is smaller, slower or restricted.

{% include section_header.liquid n=4 label='Preventing it' title='Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)' %}

Bayes: everything about task 1 sits in its posterior,

$$\log p(\theta\mid\mathcal{D}_1,\mathcal{D}_2) = \log p(\mathcal{D}_2\mid\theta) + \log p(\theta\mid\mathcal{D}_1) + \text{const}$$

Laplace: \(p(\theta\mid\mathcal{D}_1)\approx\mathcal{N}(\theta^\star, F^{-1})\), \(F\) = diagonal Fisher.

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_2(\theta) + \sum_i \frac{\lambda}{2}\,F_i\,\big(\theta_i-\theta^\star_i\big)^2$$

A spring on every weight, as stiff as task 1 is sensitive to it.

  • The Fisher approximates \(H_1\) at \(\theta^\star\): the penalty is the second-order forgetting estimate.
  • Permuted MNIST: SGD forgets, uniform L2 cannot learn, EWC keeps both. Atari games in sequence, too.
  • Limits: diagonal approximation, a \(\lambda\) to tune, springs that pile up over many tasks.
Kirkpatrick et al., PNAS 2017

In more detail

Why the Fisher, and why it works

The diagonal Fisher is \(F_i = \mathbb{E}_{x,\,y\sim p(y\mid x,\theta^\star)}\big[(\partial_{\theta_i}\log p(y\mid x,\theta^\star))^2\big]\): the average squared gradient of the model's log-likelihood, one number per weight.

For a model trained with negative log-likelihood, the Fisher information equals the expected Hessian of the loss under the model's own predictive distribution. Near a good fit it is a close, always positive semi-definite approximation to \(H_1\), and its diagonal needs only per-example gradients, which autodiff provides. With more than two tasks, one keeps a penalty per old task or a running sum of Fishers (online EWC); the anchors then accumulate and the model gradually loses plasticity.

{% include section_header.liquid n=4 label='Preventing it' title='Projection and replay, in the toy model' %}
  • GEM stores a few examples per old task and requires \(\langle g, g_k\rangle \ge 0\); if violated, it projects \(g\) onto the nearest allowed gradient. A-GEM: one averaged constraint.
  • OGD steps only orthogonally to stored gradient directions of old outputs.
  • In identity terms: GEM keeps the integrand \(\ge 0\); OGD makes it zero to first order.
  • Replay mixes a small buffer of old data into training. InstructGPT mixed in pretraining data to cut the alignment tax.
Lopez-Paz & Ranzato 2017 · Chaudhry et al. 2019 · Farajtabar et al. 2020
{% include section_header.liquid n=5 label='Fine-tuning today' title='LoRA: train a low-rank update, freeze the rest' %}
  • On GPT-3 175B: about 10,000× fewer trainable parameters and 3× less GPU memory, at full fine-tuning quality.
  • The same chain rule, now through the factorization:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_1 = -\big\langle G_1,\ BB^{\top}G_2 + G_2A^{\top}A \big\rangle$$

\(G_i = \partial\mathcal{L}_i/\partial W\). Task 2 can interfere only through rank-\(r\) projections.

  • At the start \(B = 0\), so only \(G_2A^\top A\) acts: alignment is measured inside the row space of a random \(A\).
Hu et al., ICLR 2022 (arXiv 2021)

In more detail

Deriving the LoRA expression

With \(W = W_0 + BA\), the chain rule gives \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial B = G A^{\top}\) and \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial A = B^{\top} G\). Under gradient flow on task 2, \(\dot B = -G_2A^\top\) and \(\dot A = -B^\top G_2\), so \(\dot W = \dot B A + B\dot A = -(G_2A^{\top}A + BB^{\top}G_2)\). Then \(\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_1 = \langle G_1, \dot W\rangle\) gives the formula on the slide. Compared with full fine-tuning, where \(\dot W = -G_2\), the task-2 gradient is filtered through the projections \(A^\top A\) (rank \(r\), acting on inputs) and \(BB^\top\) (rank \(r\), acting on outputs). The \(\alpha/r\) scale only rescales time.

{% include section_header.liquid n=5 label='Fine-tuning today' title='LoRA learns less, and forgets less' %}
  • Llama-2-7B trained on code and math, by continued pretraining and by instruction tuning. Learning: HumanEval, GSM8K. Forgetting: HellaSwag, ARC-Challenge, WinoGrande.
  • LoRA learned less than full fine-tuning, most clearly in continued pretraining, but kept more of the base model.
  • It forgot less than weight decay or dropout, and higher rank moved it toward full fine-tuning.
  • Full fine-tuning's weight change has 10–100× the rank of typical LoRA settings.

"Forgets less" is partly "moves less". Fair comparisons need the whole frontier.

Biderman et al., TMLR 2024
{% include section_header.liquid n=5 label='Fine-tuning today' title='2026: two views of the mechanism' %}
Per parameter · Yang et al. 2026

Freeze the weights that conflict

Injecting new facts \(\mathcal{I}\) into an LLM that has mastered facts \(\mathcal{M}\). One step changes the mastered loss by \(-\eta\sum_j s_j\), with

$$s_j = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{M}}}{\partial\theta_j}\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{I}}}{\partial\theta_j}$$
  • 50–75% of weights conflict (\(s_j < 0\)); the rest collaborate.
  • Update only weights with \(s_j \ge 0\). On Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.2 (1–7B), this learned 20–48% more new facts than baselines, with negligible forgetting.
  • It is the first-order identity, applied one weight at a time.
In attention dynamics · Koubbi et al., ICML 2026

LoRA as a perturbation of token flow

  • Toy transformer: tokens are particles on a sphere, moved by self-attention layer after layer. They form clusters that stand for the model's representations.
  • LoRA perturbs the value matrix; forgetting is drift away from the pretrained clusters.
  • Phase transitions: small perturbations stay near the clusters, larger ones escape, and drift appears beyond a critical depth. The spectral gap of the value matrix sets the threshold.
  • On Qwen3-0.6B, base-task loss jumps once the perturbation norm passes a threshold, and trained LoRA updates lock onto the dominant value directions.

Both say what EWC says: forgetting is how far you move, weighted by how sensitive that direction is.

{% include section_header.liquid n=6 label='Open questions' title='What is still open, and where my project fits' %}

Open questions

  • Which similarity? Input overlap, representation similarity (CKA), gradient alignment and Fisher overlap can disagree, and none is an agreed predictor.
  • Fair comparisons. "Forgets less" is confounded with "learns less"; few papers report frontiers.
  • Prediction, not post-mortem. Estimate before fine-tuning how much will be forgotten, and where.
  • Beyond one step. Long chains of fine-tunes, Adam's preconditioning, and LLM scale.
My course project

Predicting forgetting from gradients

  • At \(\theta^\star\), compute gradient alignment, sensitivity (diagonal Fisher, Hutchinson) and LoRA's projected alignment with torch.func.
  • Fine-tune along similarity ladders: teacher–student networks and a small pretrained LM, full fine-tuning vs. LoRA \(r \in \{4, 16, 64\}\).
  • Score each predictor against measured forgetting, on the learning–forgetting frontier.

{% include section_header.liquid label='Takeaways' title='Five things to remember' %}
  1. Forgetting comes from the optimization path, not from missing capacity. A joint solution usually exists; fine-tuning does not look for it.
  2. By the chain rule, forgetting is accumulated gradient conflict, \(-\int\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}_1,\nabla\mathcal{L}_2\rangle\,dt\). Near the old optimum it is curvature-weighted movement, \(\tfrac12\Delta\theta^\top H_1\Delta\theta\).
  3. Similarity is non-monotonic. Identical and unrelated tasks are safe; related but different tasks forget most.
  4. Every fix constrains the update. Penalize sensitive directions (EWC), remove conflicting components (GEM, OGD), replay old data, or restrict the update (LoRA). Each trades learning for remembering.
  5. All of these quantities are derivatives: gradient inner products and Hessian–vector products, which autodiff computes at the scale of real models.
{% include section_header.liquid label='Discussion' title='Questions for the class' %}
  1. If a solution that is good for both tasks exists, why does SGD not find it, and what would make it? Think about the toy model: what information about task 1 does the fine-tuning gradient carry?
  2. Is forgetting always bad? Unlearning and safety patches want targeted forgetting; benign fine-tuning that erodes safety is forgetting nobody wanted.
  3. EWC and the curvature view assume we stay near \(\theta^\star\). After a long fine-tune, should we still trust them? What would you measure to check?
