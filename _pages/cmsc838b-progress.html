CMSC 838B · Project Progress Report · November 21, 2026

Predicting forgetting from gradients: progress

A working harness for full fine-tuning and LoRA, a numerical check of the gradient identity, a 720-run teacher–student sweep, and a first GPT-2 task ladder: what they show, and what comes next.

Semih Akkoç · ECE Ph.D., University of Maryland · Instructor: Prof. Ming C. Lin

Proposal · Lecture (Nov 17) · Summary · What remains · By Dec 17

{% include section_header.liquid n=1 label='Summary' title='What has been done so far' %}

Everything promised for Nov 17–19 is in place. The GPT-2 task ladder exists as a one-seed pilot. Three findings so far:

1 · The identity holds

Summing gradient alignment and curvature over every step recovers measured forgetting to within 0.1% on all 288 runs where it was tracked. Alignment alone does not: at step size 0.1 it misses a median 18%, and by 0.3 it explains almost none.

2 · When LoRA forgets less

In the teacher–student model, LoRA forgets far less than full FT when the new task mostly needs a new readout, at nearly the same learning and at any step size. When the features themselves must change, it forgets as much or more and learns less. On GPT-2 (one seed) it did not consistently forget less.

3 · Prediction is the open part

In the teacher–student sweep, the best quantity computed before fine-tuning reaches a rank correlation of 0.53 with forgetting, while simply measuring forgetting after the first 5% of the run reaches 0.95. On GPT-2 the SGD-based predictors do not yet carry over to AdamW. Closing these gaps is the main goal for December.

Code developed

  • ts_forgetting.py: teacher–student harness in torch.func. Full FT or LoRA on the shared layer, a head per task, and exact per-step bookkeeping of the forgetting terms (forward-over-reverse Hessian–vector products).
  • sweep.py: parallel, resumable grids.
  • lm_pilot.py: GPT-2 with Transformers and PEFT. Predictors through the whole network (exact HVP by double backward, in micro-batches) and per-block reset attribution.
  • make_report.py: every figure and table on this page, regenerated from saved results.

About 1,100 lines of Python, to be released with the final report.

Ideas formed along the way

  • Measure forgetting with a frozen task-1 head. With one shared output, a student that learns task 2 simply becomes the task-2 function, so forgetting equals the distance between the two teachers and there is nothing to predict. With a head per task, forgetting is the damage done to shared features, the same protocol as the GPT-2 pilot.
  • Hold sharpness fixed across seeds. Random teacher readouts made some seeds about 3× sharper, so one step size meant different things per seed and the Taylor expansion broke down on the sharp ones. Normalizing the readouts fixed both: a small instance of the step-size point in Prof. Lin's feedback.
  • At \(\theta^\star\) the old gradient is about zero, so gradient cosine there is noise (rank correlation −0.09 to 0.28 here, and \(|\cos| < 0.02\) on GPT-2). Predictors computed before fine-tuning need curvature or a model of the early dynamics.

The identity, and when curvature matters

Every step logs the exact change in task-1 loss together with its first-order term \(\nabla\mathcal{L}_1\cdot\Delta\theta\) (gradient alignment) and second-order term \(\tfrac12\Delta\theta^{\top}H_1\Delta\theta\) (curvature). Summed over 3,000 steps, the two recover measured forgetting with a median relative error of 0.03% (largest 0.1%). Alignment alone tracks it closely at small steps and less and less as the step grows. This is the η-versus-η² trade-off from the proposal, now measured.

Forgetting against task similarity

Two teachers define the tasks: feature similarity \(\gamma\) mixes their first layers and readout similarity \(\rho\) their output weights. When readouts agree (\(\rho = 1\)), forgetting falls almost linearly with \(\gamma\), and full FT and every LoRA rank forget about the same amount. When readouts differ, full FT forgets nearly everything even at \(\gamma = 1\), where the features are identical: it rewrites the shared layer instead of only the head. LoRA does not, and the lower the rank, the less it forgets. This protocol shows no peak at intermediate similarity, unlike Lee et al. 2021; finding which design choice makes the difference is on the list below.

{% include cmsc838b-progress/fig-similarity.html %}

Is “LoRA forgets less” just “LoRA learns less”?

Comparing methods across step sizes answers this. With matching readouts, LoRA is worse on both axes: it forgets more and learns less at every step size. With different readouts, full FT forgets about 1.9 at every step size, even the smallest, while LoRA \(r = 1\) forgets 0.1–0.25 and leaves under 2% of the task-2 loss. A smaller step does not help full FT, which points to the ratio between how fast the head and the features learn rather than the step size itself. Because LoRA's \(B\) starts at zero, its first feature updates move only within the \(r\) random input directions spanned by \(A\) (out of \(N = 100\)), so the head fits first. This matches the feature-distortion argument for linear probing before fine-tuning (Kumar et al. 2022) and is the next thing to test directly.

Curvature times distance is the best single quantity across all runs, but a short probe run beats every quantity computed at \(\theta^\star\).

Setup

Inputs \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{100})\); teachers and students are two-layer networks with 4 erf hidden units. Teacher features have unit norm, and readouts are rescaled to a common norm so that every seed has the same output scale. The student learns task 1 by online SGD (batch 128, 12,000 steps), reaching a relative loss below \(10^{-3}\). Task 2 then runs for 3,000 steps at step size 0.1 unless stated otherwise, with the task-2 head initialized as a copy of the task-1 head and the task-1 head frozen. LoRA adapts the shared layer as \(U^\star + BA\) with \(A\) random (rows of norm about 1), \(B = 0\) and \(\alpha = r\). Forgetting is the rise in task-1 loss on 8,000 held-out inputs, divided by the loss of predicting zero: 1 means no better than predicting zero, and about 2 means the old head now outputs noise of the same size. The curvature predictor is \(\kappa = g_2^{\top}H_1 g_2/\|g_2\|^2\) along the first task-2 step; “curvature × distance” is \(\tfrac12\kappa D^2\) with the distance to travel estimated as \(D = 2\mathcal{L}_2/\|g_2\|\).

{% include section_header.liquid n=3 label='Results: GPT-2 pilot' title='A first real task ladder' %}

GPT-2 small was fine-tuned on AG News (90.9% accuracy) and then on one of four task-2 datasets, ordered by how far they look from AG News. New AG News data caused no forgetting; the other three cost 2–5 accuracy points, most for MNLI, the least related task. Two things already differ from the intended ladder: DBpedia, a topic task, cost full FT more than sentiment did, and LoRA \(r = 16\) did not consistently forget less (a little less on DBpedia, more on SST-2, the same on MNLI, where it also learned less).

{% include cmsc838b-progress/table-lm.html %}

Forgetting is the drop in AG News test accuracy (2,000 examples) read through the frozen task-1 head; θ* scores 90.9%. Task-2 accuracy is on held-out data. The last four columns are computed at θ* before fine-tuning: linear-probe accuracy of the old features on task 2, gradient cosine between the two tasks over all weights and over LoRA's parameters at initialization, and task-1 curvature along the task-2 gradient. One seed, so differences of a point or two are within noise.

On the predictors, gradient cosine at \(\theta^\star\) is again about zero for every pair. Curvature along the task-2 gradient ranks the pairs in nearly the reverse order of forgetting (Spearman −0.8 for full FT, −1.0 for LoRA, over four pairs): MNLI pushes along directions where task 1 is flat, but the old features are useless for it (linear probe 38%, chance 33%), so the run travels far. In the teacher–student model the distance term corrected for this. Here, however, training uses AdamW, whose per-parameter normalization decouples distance travelled from gradient size, so the distance estimate has to be redone in Adam's metric before the predictors can be scored on GPT-2. The linear-probe accuracy of the old features does better (Spearman 0.4 for full FT, 0.8 for LoRA). As for where forgetting happens: resetting one block to \(\theta^\star\) recovers the most AG News accuracy in the middle blocks (4–7) for every pair, while the curvature at \(\theta^\star\) tends to concentrate in the first blocks. They barely agree for MNLI, shown below (Spearman 0.05 across blocks), and moderately for DBpedia (0.43) and SST-2 (0.37).

Setup

GPT-2 small (124M) reads the last token through a linear head per task. Inputs are truncated to 64 tokens. Task 1 is AG News (60,000 training texts, 600 steps of batch 32, AdamW at \(2\times10^{-5}\)). Each task 2 then trains for 500 steps, with full FT at \(2\times10^{-5}\), or LoRA \(r = 16\), \(\alpha = 32\) on all attention and MLP projections at \(2\times10^{-4}\). The new heads train at \(10^{-3}\) in both. Task 2 is AG News on the other half of its training set, DBpedia-14, SST-2 or MNLI. Predictors use 64 examples per task, and the task-2 gradient is taken through a linear probe fitted on frozen features so that it is not dominated by a random head.

{% include section_header.liquid n=4 label='What remains' title='What is left to do' %}
  • Test the mechanism behind finding 2. Run full FT with a smaller step on the shared layer only, or with a linear probe first. If that matches LoRA \(r = 1\), the effect comes from how fast the head learns relative to the features, not from low rank.
  • Close the prediction gap. Forecast the first few percent of fine-tuning from \(\theta^\star\) in each method's own parametrization (a linearized, NTK-style model). That is what the 5% probe measures directly, at a fraction of its cost.
  • Find the intermediate-similarity peak reported by Lee et al. by varying how the task-2 head is initialized and how wide the student is.
  • GPT-2 study at full size. Four ladder levels × {full FT, LoRA \(r = 4, 16, 64\)} × 3 seeds, with a learning-rate sweep so that methods are compared on the learning–forgetting frontier. The pilot above is one seed, one rank and one step size.
  • Predictor scorecard on real tasks. Redo the distance and curvature terms in AdamW's preconditioned metric, then score every predictor across task pairs against the two baselines from the proposal: representation similarity (CKA) and Fisher task embeddings (Task2Vec). The Fisher baseline only makes sense here: in the teacher–student model both tasks share one input distribution, so the model's Fisher is the same for both.
  • Per-block attribution for LoRA as well as full FT, over several seeds.
  • A causal test. Protect the blocks or parameters with the highest predicted sensitivity (freeze them, or project the task-2 gradient away from them) and compare with protecting a random set of the same size, at matched task-2 accuracy.
  • Step size, following Prof. Lin's comment. Sweep the task-1 and task-2 step sizes separately, estimate \(\lambda_{\max}\) at \(\theta^\star\) by power iteration on Hessian–vector products, and repeat the alignment-versus-curvature split on GPT-2.
  • Final report and code release.
